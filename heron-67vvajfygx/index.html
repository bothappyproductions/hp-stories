What you told us

Your goals, and where you’re starting

Two goals in your own words, two channels you’ve just opened, and two things we noticed on heroncare.org. Each one maps to a piece of content or a channel further down.

Goal · Understanding
Increase understanding and awareness of the benefit of nonprofit, community-based health navigation.

Health navigation is a service most people can’t picture. Ninety seconds of a navigator at a kitchen table explains it better than a page of text.

Goal · Honor
Honor the people living with complex health conditions through their emotive stories.

You already call them Heron Heroes. Each one gets a short documentary that opens on their life, not their diagnosis.

Starting point · Social

Facebook and LinkedIn pages, just launched

A new page is an advantage: every post from day one can follow one plan, with nothing old to clean up.

Starting point · Email

A targeted email campaign, planned

Email is where a worried caregiver reads at 11pm. Build the sequence around video content and every email has a reason to be opened.

What we noticed · The website

Strong words, no one on screen

heroncare.org explains the service clearly, but apart from Chris’s headshot it shows no people and no navigation in action. Families are deciding whether to trust a stranger with their care.

What we noticed · Proof

One testimonial, carrying a lot

Weston’s family quote (“he came with a plan”) is the most persuasive line on the site. On camera, that family could do far more for Heron than a quote can.

ROI

We don’t make video for its own sake. Every piece of content below has a job, a place it lives, and a number it should move: calls booked, care teams referring, donors giving, families who stay.

For a new nonprofit every dollar counts, so the ideas below are a starting point for a conversation about what Heron actually needs across its website, Facebook, LinkedIn and email.

Need 02 · Honoring the people you serve

Heron Heroes, in their own words

Your site ends with one line from Weston’s family. Heron Heroes turns lines like that into short documentaries: people or families, recorded at home doing what they love, telling what changed when someone came with a plan. Your own phrase is the series rule: we are not defined by a diagnosis.

  • Consent first. Written releases, a plain-language explanation of where the video will run, and the right to withdraw before release.
  • Their review before anyone else’s. Every subject sees their cut before it is published.
  • No clinical detail without permission. We capture life at home, not exam rooms, and name a condition only if the person wants it named.
$

What it moves: trust. Families decide faster when they meet someone like them, and these stories are the heart of the email sequence and the GivingTuesday appeal.

Need 03 · Why Heron exists

Born to Advocate: the story behind Heron

A boy with a debilitating form of autism, a mother who would not stop advocating, and the career that followed.
